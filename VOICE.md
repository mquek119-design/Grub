# Grub — Voice & Tone Guide

This is the reference for how Grub talks. Read this before writing any
user-facing copy — buttons, notifications, empty states, error messages,
onboarding text. `CLAUDE.md`'s "The voice" section is the constitution this
guide expands on; nothing here overrides it. Where the two disagree, CLAUDE.md
wins.

---

## The Split: 70/30, and it is deliberate

**Grub's default voice is dry, plain, British, faintly deadpan** — the
organised housemate who says the true thing once and moves on, not the one
performing for the group chat. That's the voice on roughly seven screens out
of ten, and on every money screen without exception.

**A louder, funnier register is allowed on a narrow, named set of screens** —
empty states, onboarding, milestones — because those are the only places
nothing else is competing for the reader's attention and a joke can't be
mistaken for a figure. That's the remaining three in ten. It never spreads
past that list by default; a new screen is dry until named otherwise below.

Both registers are **British**. The loud lane is not licence to write like a
US TikTok caption — no "no cap," no "bestie" stacked on every line, no
exclamation marks (never, in either register — energy comes from word choice,
not punctuation). A joke that needs a UK reader to translate it first has
failed the same test a confusing sentence has.

---

## The Core Rule

**Brain rot is seasoning, not the dish.** One bit of personality per screen,
maximum, and only on a screen from the loud list below. Never on money
screens — that part is absolute, not a majority rule. Every line must be
instantly readable at 11pm on a phone screen; if someone has to read it twice
to get the joke, it's failed regardless of how funny it is.

If a line is doing personality and doesn't also carry real information (what
happened, what to do next, how much money), cut it. Voice is the seasoning on
top of a clear message — it never replaces the message.

---

## DO

- **Be specific over vague.** "Swap the Lurpak for own-brand" beats "consider
  alternatives." "You've got chicken and peppers from Thursday" beats "you
  have ingredients available."
- **Use pounds, not feelings.** "£4.28 saved" not "save money." "You owe Sam
  £8.50" not "you have an outstanding balance."
- **Name people.** "Mike hasn't paid" not "1 payment outstanding." Social
  visibility is the enforcement mechanism — use it.
- **Acknowledge the awkward.** The app exists inside real housemate dynamics —
  nudging someone to pay, admitting you forgot to cook. Write like you know
  this is a bit awkward, because it is.
- **Stay instantly readable.** Someone reading this is tired, hungry, or
  checking their phone mid-conversation. One pass, message lands.

## DON'T

- **No corporate speak.** Never "please," never "successfully," never "we
  apologize for the inconvenience."
- **No exclamation marks.** Ever. On either register. Enthusiasm reads as fake
  almost immediately — let the words carry the energy.
- **No try-hard slang, in the loud lane or anywhere else.** If a line reads
  like an adult reaching for whatever's trending, cut it. The loud register is
  dry wit turned up, not a different vocabulary borrowed from somewhere else.
- **No lecturing.** Don't scold about food waste, spending habits, or unpaid
  balances. State the fact, let the social pressure do the work.
- **No hedging.** Don't soften bad news with "unfortunately" or "we're sorry
  to say." Just say what happened.
- **Never decorate money screens.** Payment amounts, balances, and split
  breakdowns get zero jokes, on either register. Clarity only. This is the one
  rule the 70/30 split does not touch.

---

## Where each register applies

| Screen type | Register | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Money / split / payments / balances | Dry only, minimal even for that | Numbers must be instantly clear. Absolute — see CLAUDE.md. |
| Basket / checkout | Dry | Functional; one dry line of character at most. |
| Settings / account | Dry, no voice at all | Admin, not conversation. |
| Cooking / rota / reminders | Dry | Practical instruction, read while hands are busy. |
| Errors | Dry, acknowledges the annoyance | Get out of the way with the fix fast. |
| **Empty states** | Loud allowed | Nothing else on the screen competes for attention, and there's no figure nearby to undercut. |
| **Onboarding** | Loud allowed | Sets the tone for the whole app once, at the start. |
| **Milestones / streaks** | Loud allowed | A one-off moment, not a screen someone reads every day. |
| Notifications (non-money) | Dry, occasional flourish | Read at a glance; personality only where it doesn't cost a beat of clarity. |

## Example Lines by Situation

### Onboarding *(loud lane)*
- Welcome: "You're in. Now get the rest of the house on here before someone buys a fourth bag of pasta."
- Invite: "Send this to the group chat. Yes, even the one who has cereal for dinner. Especially them."

### Empty States *(loud lane)*
- Empty plan: "Nobody's picked anything. You're all just going to wing it again aren't you."
- Empty pantry: "Your cupboard is giving nothing. Probably accurate."
- No recipes saved: "Absolutely nothing here. Paste a link or write one out — it's the one bit you can't skip."
- No savings yet: "No receipts yet. Finish your first shop and we start counting."
- No balance history: "Clean slate. No one owes anyone anything. Enjoy it while it lasts."

### Planning *(dry)*
- Cutoff countdown label: "Hours until lock"
- Cutoff approaching: "3 hours left. After that you're eating whatever everyone else picked."
- Housemate hasn't submitted: "Sam hasn't picked anything yet."

### Living Mode — post-order *(dry)*
- Skipped meal: "Skipped tonight's plan. You've got chicken and peppers left — here's what else you can make."
- Perishable warning: "Chicken from yesterday — use by tomorrow."
- End-of-week nudge: "Still got rice and onions sitting there. Use them or they're going in the bin."
- Pantry match: "You've already got pasta and cheddar. This recipe only needs cream and garlic."

### Basket *(dry, one line max)*
- Basket ready: "23 items, £42.50. Everyone fed for the week."
- Over budget: "£7 over. Swap the Lurpak for own-brand and you're there."
- Under delivery minimum: "£8 short of the delivery minimum. Add a couple of snacks, or collect instead."
- Own-brand toggle: "Own-brand mode: on."
- Collector hasn't ordered: "Basket's been sat there since Sunday. Jordan — your move."

### Split & Payments *(dry, absolute)*
- Split posted: "This week's total: you owe Sarah £31.40."
- Breakdown intro: "Here's the working, item by item."
- Delivery fee line: "Delivery: £3.50 — £0.88 each."
- I've Paid confirmation: "Marked as paid."
- Payment nudge to the house: "Mike still hasn't paid."
- Payment nudge to the debtor: "You owe Sarah £8.50 from Thursday's shop."
- All settled: "Everyone's paid up."

### Savings *(dry, one loud line only if it's a genuine milestone)*
- Weekly: "Saved £4.20 this week — one bag of rice covered three meals."
- Cumulative milestone: "Your house has saved £142 this term."

### Cooking & Rota *(dry)*
- Cooking reminder: "It's your turn tonight. Recipe's here."

### Leftovers *(dry, light touch allowed — it's an empty-state-adjacent board)*
- Leftover added: "Chilli — feeds 2, made Monday. Clock's ticking."

### One-off Purchases *(dry)*
- Logged: "Added. They owe you for this."

### Streaks & Milestones *(loud lane, sparing — a one-off moment, not every week)*
- Streak: "4 weeks straight of everyone actually paying on time."

### Errors *(dry, acknowledge then fix)*
- Session expired: "Tesco logged you out. The collector needs to sign back in — takes about 30 seconds."
- Slot gone: "Thursday 6–8 got taken. Next one's Friday 7–9."
- Generic error: "Something broke. Try again in a moment."

### Destructive Actions *(dry, plain, no wit near a warning)*
- Leave house: "If you leave, your share of this week's shop stays. Outstanding debts follow you."
- Delete account: "This deletes everything — recipes, history, the lot. No going back."

## Writing Test Before Shipping Any Copy

Ask, in order:
1. Does this say something specific and true (a number, a name, a real state)?
2. Would someone understand it in one read at 11pm?
3. Is this a money screen? If yes — dry only, stop here.
4. Is this screen on the loud list (empty state, onboarding, milestone)? If
   not, stay dry.
5. Is there already a joke somewhere else on this screen? One, maximum.
6. Would a British reader get it without translating US slang first?
7. Does it sound like a person, not a brand trying to sound like a person?

If it fails any of these, rewrite before shipping.
